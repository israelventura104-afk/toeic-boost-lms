Word Forms

A full 60-minute class on one skill: decide whether the blank needs a noun, verb, adjective, or adverb — then choose the matching form from the same word family.

~60 min class 5 demos to model 15 scored items Feedback at the end

Teach the rule · 15–20 min

What Word Forms are really testing

In TOEIC Part 5, many items look like vocabulary questions. They are not. The four options often share the same root (decide / decision / decisive / decisively). Your job is not to translate the whole sentence first. Your job is to name the grammatical slot of the blank, then choose the form that fits that slot.

The four slots

Noun

Names a person, thing, idea, or process. Often after a / an / the / this / their, or after a preposition.

the decision · her professionalism · for development
Verb

Shows the action or state. Follows a subject or a modal / auxiliary, and carries tense or agreement.

will decide · has decided · to develop
Adjective

Describes a noun. Sits before a noun or after linking verbs like be / seem / remain.

a decisive manager · the report is complete
Adverb

Describes a verb, an adjective, or a whole idea. Often ends in -ly, but not always.

answered promptly · highly successful · work efficiently

How to decide the slot (the method)

  1. Ignore A–D for three seconds. Look only at the words immediately before and after the blank.
  2. Ask: what job does this blank do? Naming (noun), doing (verb), describing a noun (adjective), or describing how / when / to what degree (adverb)?
  3. Match the ending to the job. Common patterns: -tion / -ment / -ness / -ity (noun); -ive / -al / -able / -ous (adjective); -ly (adverb); base / -s / -ed / -ing (verb forms).
  4. Eliminate by structure, not by “sounds nice.” A familiar word in the wrong slot is still wrong.

Fast signals (teach these as habits)

  • Article / possessive + blank + noun? → often adjective (a ____ proposal).
  • Article / possessive + blank (end of phrase)? → often noun (show ____ / her ____).
  • Subject + blank + object / complement? → verb.
  • Verb already present, blank nearby? → often adverb (spoke ____, ____ important).
  • Preposition + blank? → usually a noun phrase (for ____, of ____).
Teacher move for a 60-minute class

Board only the four slots and 2–3 signals first. Do not open the full strategy library. When she hesitates, ask one question only: “Noun, verb, adjective, or adverb?” — then look at options.

Common traps

  • Choosing a noun because it “feels serious” when the blank needs an adverb.
  • Choosing -ing / -ed forms without checking whether a tensed verb is required.
  • Translating from Spanish first (that delays the slot check and invites look-alike errors).

Five demos — think aloud with your student
